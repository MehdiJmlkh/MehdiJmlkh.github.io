Large language models (LLMs) often exhibit mode collapse in open-ended generation, repeatedly producing similar responses despite the availability of diverse valid alternatives. Existing approaches address this problem through diversity-aware optimization or decoding-time interventions, often requiring curated preference data, candidate selection, or additional inference. We adopt on-policy unlearning, a general framework that dynamically constructs unlearning targets from the model's current policy, and introduce UnMode to mitigate response mode collapse. At each round, UnMode samples responses from the current model and updates its parameters to reduce the probability of generating them. Resampling after each update lets the forget targets track the evolving policy, progressively suppressing likely responses and making alternatives relatively more probable. This approach requires neither curated replacement data nor separately sampled alternative responses. We evaluate UnMode on two open-ended generation benchmarks and code-completion tasks. Results show that UnMode improves response diversity and utility over diversity-oriented baselines. It also improves pass@5 and pass@10 on code completion, increasing the likelihood of finding correct solutions when sampling multiple candidates. These findings demonstrate the potential of on-policy unlearning as a simple and data-efficient approach to reducing response mode collapse in LLMs.
